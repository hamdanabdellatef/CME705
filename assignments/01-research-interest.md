# Assignment 1: Research-interest memo

## Purpose

Turn broad interests into two directions that can be investigated through data and evidence. This memo begins the semester research project. It does not commit you to a final topic.

## Task

Identify two application or methodological areas that you may want to investigate. For each area, address:

1. the affected system, process, or population;
2. the decision or understanding sought;
3. a plausible unit of observation;
4. the likely ML task;
5. possible data and how they might be accessed;
6. a simple baseline;
7. one difficulty, risk, or source of misleading evidence; and
8. one question you would investigate next.

Conclude by selecting one area to explore first. Explain why it offers a useful and feasible starting point.

Use the [Week 1 problem-formulation canvas](../modules/01-ml-foundations/worksheet.md) before writing.

## Sources

Use at least two credible starting sources in total. A source may establish the application need, describe a dataset, or provide a relevant method. Link or cite the exact source. You do not need to complete a full literature review for this assignment.

## Deliverable

- 500–700 words;
- a descriptive title;
- headings for Direction A, Direction B, and Initial Choice;
- at least two cited starting sources; and
- a short disclosure of any automated tool used for searching, coding, or writing, following the policy for the current offering.

## Assessment

| Criterion | Points | Strong evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Application and motivation | 5 | Names a concrete system or population and explains why the question matters |
| ML formulation | 5 | Identifies a plausible observation, task, input, and output |
| Feasibility | 4 | Discusses realistic data access and a simple baseline |
| Difficulty and judgment | 4 | Identifies a specific risk, ambiguity, or evaluation challenge |
| Sources and communication | 2 | Uses traceable sources and concise, precise writing |

## Submission check

Before submitting, confirm:

- each direction begins with a problem rather than a model name;
- the proposed data could contain the stated unit and target;
- the baseline is simpler than the proposed direction;
- the difficulty is specific enough to affect an experiment; and
- the conclusion explains why one direction should be investigated first.
