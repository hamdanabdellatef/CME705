# CME705 final presentation rubric

Score the observable presentation and questions. Do not infer missing evidence from earlier supervision.

| Criterion | Weight | Strong evidence | Developing evidence | Insufficient evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| question and contribution | 15 | precise question, relevant motivation, bounded contribution | question is understandable but scope or contribution is partly vague | topic or model is named without a testable question |
| experimental protocol | 15 | population, data, split, baseline, metric, selection, and controls are clear | several protocol elements are present but one material choice is ambiguous | audience cannot determine how the evidence was produced |
| central evidence | 25 | focused result, uncertainty or observations, provenance, and test status support the title | result is relevant but uncertainty, provenance, or comparison is incomplete | claims rely on screenshots, isolated numbers, or unsupported examples |
| interpretation and limitations | 15 | observation and inference are distinct; limitation names affected claim and consequence | a limitation is named but does not materially bound the conclusion | conclusion exceeds evidence or ignores a material threat |
| reproducibility and release | 10 | fixed identifier, command, expected output, agreement rule, and access route are stated | repository is identified but reproduction route is incomplete | submitted artifact cannot be located or central result is untraceable |
| visual and oral communication | 10 | readable, accessible slides; coherent transitions; talk finishes within the announced time | story is mostly clear but density, labels, or timing interferes | audience cannot follow or inspect the main evidence |
| question defense | 5 | answers use observation, interpretation, uncertainty, and next evidence | answer is relevant but omits an evidence boundary | answer is invented, evasive, or materially contradicts the artifact |
| research direction | 5 | observed boundary leads to competing explanations, discriminating experiment, and decision rule | next experiment is feasible but does not clearly separate explanations | future work is only a model name or broad aspiration |
| **Total** | **100** | | | |

## Scoring anchors

- **90–100:** coherent and defensible contribution; evidence and artifact can be inspected directly.
- **80–89:** sound project with limited ambiguity or presentation weakness.
- **70–79:** meaningful work, but one central link among question, protocol, evidence, or conclusion is incomplete.
- **60–69:** substantial material is present, yet the main claim cannot be defended reliably.
- **Below 60:** central evidence, artifact access, or authorship understanding is missing.

Apply institutional grade conversion separately.

## Essential conditions

A presentation cannot receive full credit when it presents validation evidence as an untouched final test, reports published results as project-generated, omits the data source, cannot identify the central result, includes prohibited material, relies on model complexity as the contribution, or exceeds the announced time policy.
