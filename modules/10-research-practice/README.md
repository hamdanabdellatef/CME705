# Research practice

**Guiding question:** How can an experiment support a precise claim, survive independent review, and motivate the next research question?

## Learning outcomes

- connect each claim to traceable experimental evidence;
- search and compare relevant literature;
- separate hypotheses, observations, summaries, and inferences;
- design baselines, ablations, and robustness checks;
- record code, data, configurations, environments, and results reproducibly;
- calculate and interpret repeated-run uncertainty;
- identify construct, internal, external, and statistical threats to validity;
- reproduce a result from a fixed artifact package;
- write specific peer-review comments and verify revisions;
- propose a feasible next experiment or research direction.

## Topics

- research questions and claim scope
- literature synthesis and source attribution
- artifact identity and provenance
- repeatability, reproducibility, and replicability
- repeated runs, pairing, and uncertainty
- experiment manifests and integrity hashes
- ablations, robustness, and threats to validity
- scientific writing and limitations
- peer reproduction and review comments
- evidence-based future research directions

## Evidence of learning

A peer can start from the submitted repository state, run the declared command, locate the expected output, compare it under a prespecified tolerance, and understand which claim the result supports. The final report distinguishes observed evidence from interpretation and names a feasible experiment that could change the conclusion.

## Place in the course

Week 13 prepares the project for independent review. Week 14 presents and defends the verified evidence, freezes the final release, and turns a specific limitation into a feasible next study. Consult the [course schedule](../../schedule.md) and [project brief](../../research-project/brief.md).
